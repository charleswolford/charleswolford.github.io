Case studyRidge and Partners LLP · 2025 – present

Estate digital twins

One view of an estate: buildings, floors, assets and survey data together in interactive 2D and 3D, instead of spread across PDFs, CAD files and spreadsheets.

Role
Solutions lead, end to end
Sectors
Healthcare, higher education, housing
Platform
ArcGIS Online

cs.before()

Before

Estate information arrived the way most estates hold it: PDFs of drawings, CAD files and spreadsheets, each kept somewhere different.

Finding a single fact took time. Using two datasets together, such as an asset register alongside a floor plan, was harder still, because nothing tied them to a shared view of the estate.

Workflow

  1. 01 · Inputs

    Four sources

    • Revit models
    • Drone captures
    • Survey data
    • Asset registers

  2. 02 · Georeference

    Placed on the grid

    • Revit survey points resolved
    • Models land on the British National Grid
    • Lined up with survey and drone data

  3. 03 · Integrate

    One environment

    • BIM and floor plan pipelines
    • Asset records brought in alongside
    • Published to ArcGIS

  4. 04 · Output

    Estate experience

    • Interactive 2D and 3D views
    • Buildings, floors and assets together
    • One place to search

Diagram of the workflow, not client data.

The hard part

A Revit model only lands in the right place on a map if its survey point is set up correctly. I resolved the survey point georeferencing so the models land correctly on the British National Grid, lined up with the survey and drone data around them. Without that, nothing else in the view can be trusted.

Outcome

  • One place to look

    Information that took time to track down now sits in a single view of the estate.

  • Data used together

    BIM, drone, survey and asset data can be read side by side, where before each lived in its own file.

  • Evidence-led planning

    Part of the digital estate roadmaps I shape with clients, moving them from fragmented data to confident planning.
